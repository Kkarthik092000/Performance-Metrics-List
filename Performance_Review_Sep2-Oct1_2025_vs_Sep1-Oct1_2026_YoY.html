Revenue up 11.1% year over year, but growth is volume‑led — conversion and productivity both fell.

With 26 working days in both periods, revenue grew from ₹148.20L (Sep 2 – Oct 1, 2025) to ₹164.64L (Sep 1 – Oct 1, 2026), up 11.09%.         Key Takeaways

        The headline story reading all tables — including the market and daily trend data — together.
        +11.09%

        Real growth on equal working days. Revenue grew from ₹148.20L to ₹164.64L, and Revenue/Day from ₹5,69,999 to ₹6,33,231, with 26 working days in both periods.

        P to C -3.31pp

        Pitches surged but conversion fell. Pitches grew 48.39% (17,509→25,981) while Pitch‑to‑Close fell from 14.24% to 10.93%; only North Cluster (+3.63pp) improved, and Nagpur (−20.34pp) and WCC (−8.89pp) fell the most.

        Conv -1.28pp

        Conversion Rate eased (7.41%→6.13%) — Law + New Businesses (+1.66pp) was the only region to improve; Nagpur fell 11.28pp as its funnel broadened sharply.

        Prod -18.21%

        Headcount outran revenue. Head Count grew 35.83% (120→163) against 11.09% revenue growth, so Productivity (Revenue per Boe) fell from ₹1,23,500 to ₹1,01,006; every region's Productivity fell except PackHouse inside South.

        South +₹17.65L

        South Cluster added the most revenue. It grew 40.47% (₹43.61L→₹61.26L) to 37.2% of total revenue, led by FightClub (+51.40%) and PackHouse (+75.77%) even after South Core went inactive.

        North +67.28%

        Fastest growth: North Cluster and Law + New Businesses. North grew 67.28% (₹10.88L→₹18.20L) with the only P to C improvement; Law + New Businesses grew 63.67% (₹24.27L→₹39.72L) with the only Conversion Rate improvement.

        WCC -78.19%

        WCC Cluster collapsed. Revenue fell from ₹35.62L to ₹7.77L as Head Count fell from 26 to 12 — a ₹27.85L drag that cut the year‑over‑year gain to 11.09% from a potential 30%.

        New 59.19%

        Growth came from new colleges. 402 colleges with no revenue in the prior‑year period supplied 59.19% of this year's revenue, while only 148 of 446 colleges repeated — and repeat‑college revenue fell 6.74%.
