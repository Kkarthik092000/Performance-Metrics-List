Oct 1 – 17, 2025 → Oct 5 – 20, 2026 · Year‑over‑Year · 13 working days each

Revenue up 17.1% year over year on equal working days, as a 62% jump in leads converted at a lower rate.

With 13 working days in each period, revenue grew from ₹59.14L (Oct 1 – 17, 2025) to ₹69.24L (Oct 5 – 20, 2026), up 17.08% — a ₹10.10L gain, or ₹4,54,885 → ₹5,32,577 per working day. Leads rose 62.01%, campaigns 53.67% and pitches 66.55%, but Conversion Rate fell from 6.80% to 5.30%, P to C from 13.14% to 9.96% and Average Order Value 7.24% (₹6,010→₹5,574), so units grew only 26.22% (984→1,242). Head Count grew 18.18% (121→143), leaving Productivity (Revenue per Boe) essentially flat at −0.93%. Medical Cluster (+206.05%, +₹9.03L) drove almost 90% of the net gain, while WCC Cluster (−85.39%, −₹7.83L) was the only region to decline. This report covers the overall funnel, all six regions, the South Cluster sub‑clusters, market‑level revenue, and day‑by‑day trends.

        Reading the daily pattern

        • The 2025 daily table runs Oct 2 – 17 (16 days; no Oct 1 rows were supplied) and the 2026 table Oct 5 – 20 (16 days), so Day 1 is Oct 2, 2025 and Oct 5, 2026 and the lines compare one‑to‑one. Both periods have 13 working days: in 2025 Oct 2 (Day 1) and Sundays Oct 5 (Day 4) and Oct 12 (Day 11) had no sales; in 2026 Sunday Oct 11 (Day 7), Oct 19 (Day 15) and Oct 20 (Day 16) had none, while Sunday Oct 18 was worked (₹5.66L).
        • 2025 revenue had one outsized spike: Oct 4 produced ₹8.99L (the 2025 peak), including ₹3.91L from WCC; otherwise 2025 working days ran ₹3.0L–₹5.6L. 2026 was steadier and higher — ₹3.4L–₹7.8L, with eight of 13 working days above ₹5.0L and a peak of ₹7.76L on Oct 10.
        • Low points recurring roughly every 7 days are Sundays — campaigns and pitching pause, so revenue and campaigns drop close to zero while leads keep trickling in.

        Insights

        Key Takeaways

        The headline story reading all tables — including the market and daily trend data — together.
        +17.08%

        Real growth on equal working days. Revenue grew from ₹59.14L to ₹69.24L (+₹10.10L) and Revenue/Day from ₹4,54,885 to ₹5,32,577, with 13 working days in each period.

        Units +26% vs Leads +62%

        Volume is outrunning output. Leads grew 62.01%, campaigns 53.67% and pitches 66.55%, but units grew only 26.22% and AOV fell from ₹6,010 to ₹5,574 — more effort per rupee earned.

        Conv -1.50pp

        Conversion Rate fell (6.80%→5.30%) — Law + New Businesses (−17.13pp, distorted by a very small 2025 lead base), Nagpur (−2.24pp), North (−1.44pp) and South (−1.30pp) all fell; only Medical improved (+2.31pp).

        P to C -3.18pp

        Pitch‑to‑Close weakened (13.14%→9.96%) despite 66.55% more pitches; Medical rose 4.88pp to 12.69% while Nagpur (−10.24pp), South (−1.71pp) and North (−1.91pp) fell.

        Medical +206.05%

        Medical Cluster is the growth engine. Revenue rose from ₹4.38L to ₹13.405L (+₹9.03L, about 89% of the net gain) with Productivity up 139.52% — it is now 19.4% of revenue, from 7.4%.

        North +47.92%

        North Cluster grew almost 50%. Revenue rose from ₹6.01L to ₹8.89L as headcount grew from 15 to 22 — and Productivity held at ₹40,409, the only region to sustain output per person on a bigger team.

        WCC -85.39%

        WCC Cluster is the only decline. Revenue fell from ₹9.17L to ₹1.34L (−₹7.83L) as Head Count fell from 22 to 6 — a loss equal to about 44% of the gains in the other regions; 27% of the 2025 base was one ₹2.5L unlisted entry.

        South +22.93%

        South grew, but on a much bigger team. FightClub (+41.15%) and PackHouse (+21.61%) added ₹5.97L, South Core's ₹1.28L exit trimmed the net to ₹4.68L, and Head Count rising 33→51 cut Productivity by 20.46%.

        Prod -0.93%

        Headcount grew in step with revenue. Head Count rose 18.18% (121→143) against 17.08% revenue growth, so Productivity (Revenue per Boe) edged from ₹48,872 to ₹48,416.

        New 76.63%

        Three‑quarters of revenue came from new colleges. 309 colleges with no prior‑year revenue supplied 76.63% of revenue while only 58 of 251 colleges repeated — and repeat‑college revenue fell 6.40%.
